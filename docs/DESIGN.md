# Interface direction

The music is the primary surface. App controls should feel like compact
rehearsal tools surrounding a clean music stand.

Palette: paper white #FFFFFF, interface gray #F2F4F6, ink #18242C,
rehearsal blue #285E79, muted gray #63717B and a restrained active mark
#D59B2D. Keep yellow for the active position, not decoration.

Use native San Francisco for controls, with rounded numerals for tempo and a
clear title scale. Preserve the score's own notation and typography. Do not
introduce display fonts into the score or style the app as a marketing page.

On a full-size iPad, a narrow library sidebar sits beside a broad score area.
Each musical system has a small label and timing control, with most space
reserved for the original notation. Portrait can hide the sidebar. Use native
toolbars and sheets for preparation controls.

Perform mode will show the current system and upcoming context with one compact
transport. Count-in, beat pulses and automatic focus movement have a musical
purpose. Respect Reduce Motion and keep touch targets usable at a music stand.

Review: the initial layout uses a continuous score surface, not equal dashboard
cards or decorative gradients. Sample notation is original content and remains
the main visual element.
